This was a quick win with one of my favorite tools, the 3D Printer. The majority of my 3D printing use is functional.
I print jigs, fixtures, and fixes for projects I am working on. I've also fixed a few things around the house by
creating a quick CAD model and letting my Bambu X1C spit it out. A few of the hard-to-find parts on my E30
are 3D printed from ABS plastic, and I can't count the number of small fixtures I use for fabrication that are made
using that same process. To say I'm a fan of 3D printing is a slight understatement.

I recently purchased a set of [Harbor Freight Daytona](https://www.harborfreight.com/3-ton-aluminum-jack-stands-56687.html) jack stands. 
I am a big fan of the Schwaben-style stands. They have a pin locking mechanism instead of the traditional cam-
lock style stands. The Daytonas are nearly an identical copy of the Schwabens, at least visually. I just needed a 
set of jack pads for them as I don't want to set my fragile E30 on the stand directly.

The pads are relatively simple designs: a round puck with a few reliefs for the pinch weld and the jack's edges. I had a
model whipped up and off to the printer to print my first version. These were printed with [Bambu TPU for AMS](https://store.bblcdn.com/s5/default/0a98353dce6d486ca848b21d2b19a207.pdf).
I don't believe this will be as durable as the rubber ones, but they got me through the weekend so I can get some on order.
